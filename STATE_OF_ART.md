# State of the art and research synthesis

Evidence cut-off and access date: **11 September 2026**. This is a decision-oriented research foundation, not a systematic-review claim. The [search record](research/SEARCH_LOG.md) documents coverage and limitations; the [bibliography](research/BIBLIOGRAPHY.md) distinguishes full-text, methods, code, abstract, and indexed-excerpt inspection. No scientific result has been internally reproduced.

## What the evidence supports us doing

The strongest near-term question is whether biological wiring supplies useful constraints after dynamics, learning, normalization, and resource allocation are controlled. Larger graphs and realistic bodies are available, but they do not remove these identification problems. Begin with three discriminating assays: a recurrent computational-capacity test, a fixed-wiring adaptive-learning test, and controlled expansion of a learning circuit. They are specified in [EXPERIMENTS.md](EXPERIMENTS.md). These are proposed experiments, not established findings.

The literature already contains connectome-based reservoirs, learned visual dynamics, reinforcement-learning mushroom-body models, and structural perturbation studies. Consequently, using a fly graph in an artificial network, fitting its dynamics, or changing its connections is not sufficient novelty. A credible contribution would isolate a robust mechanism, expose a confound in existing comparisons, or establish a reproducible capability–resource relationship under unusually strong controls. Whole-adult structured enlargement with broad held-out evaluation remains a candidate gap: **no equivalent located in the documented search**, rather than guaranteed originality.

## 1. Connectomes, chemistry, and biological variation

Adult datasets have changed the feasible questions. FlyWire provides a dense adult-female brain reconstruction; its companion annotation effort connects morphology, cell typing, and comparisons with other reconstructions. These resources support circuit selection and robustness tests, while also documenting the distinction between stereotyped cell types and variation in numbers or connection strengths. A graph snapshot and an annotation release are different objects and must be versioned separately. [S01](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07558-y), [S02](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07686-5)

BANC extends reconstruction across the brain and nerve cord, making brain–body pathways more accessible. However, its published methods describe missing lamina, R1–R6 and ocellar structures, as well as damage affecting antennal inputs. It is therefore a promising structural reference for particular sensorimotor circuits, not a complete specification of every sensory channel. A brain-and-cord dataset does not by itself solve sensory transduction, muscles, or physiology. [S03](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10735-w)

Provider reconciliation matters now: Janelia advertises MaleCNS v1.0, whereas the accessed Codex FAQ lists MCNS v0.9. Treat those as different releases until actual exports and filtering rules are reconciled. The same FAQ identifies FAFB v783 and BANC v888. Never combine a provider's neuron count with another provider's connection table merely because the specimen name matches. The authoritative release/access matrix is in [DATA_PROVENANCE.md](DATA_PROVENANCE.md). [S04](https://male-cns.janelia.org/), [S05](https://codex.flywire.ai/faq), [S06](https://male-cns.janelia.org/download/)

Smaller datasets remain scientifically useful. The published larval mushroom-body matrix offers a compact, inspectable learning-circuit input. Our static audit identifies a 40-PN/73-mature-KC left-side projection for the first learning assays. This is a chosen circuit boundary, not the whole larval mushroom body. The complete supplementary matrix has duplicate display labels and some unequal row/column ordering; the audit preserves explicit indices. Its synapse counts are aggregated anatomical contacts, not measured conductances. [S11](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature23455), [S64](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fnature23455/MediaObjects/41586_2017_BFnature23455_MOESM3_ESM.zip)

Chemical annotations reduce uncertainty but do not eliminate it. EM-based neurotransmitter prediction is a classification result with errors and coverage limits. Receptor expression, cotransmission, compartment, neuromodulation, and state can alter effective influence; a neurotransmitter label is not a universally valid sign or time constant. Transcriptomic resources can inform plausible model families but require a defensible mapping between recorded cell identity and connectomic type. We have screened that route, not solved such a mapping. [S27](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0092867424003076), [S28](https://elifesciences.org/articles/50901), [S47](https://github.com/flyconnectome/drosophila_neurotransmitters)

Cross-animal comparisons offer a natural test of whether a putative architectural benefit depends on one reconstruction. Descending/ascending-neuron comparisons are particularly relevant to later embodiment, but matched cell types are not independent experimental replicates of a whole-brain manipulation. Sex, age, developmental stage, segmentation, completeness, and annotation procedures remain confounded unless explicitly separated. [S54](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-08925-z)

**Implication:** use small pinned inputs to establish a measurement method; require an adult, independently reconstructed circuit before interpreting a larval result as evidence about adult scaling. Test sensitivity to low-count-edge thresholds and uncertain signs as alternative data-generating assumptions, not as opportunities to select the best test result.

## 2. Neural dynamics and model fidelity

An anatomical connection constrains a potential interaction. It does not uniquely specify its causal effect in a behaving animal. The effectome perspective makes this gap central: observing or predicting circuit influence requires interventions and functional information beyond static adjacency. We therefore treat synapse-count weights, signs, neuronal equations, and plasticity rules as distinct assumptions with independent validation needs. [S09](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07982-0)

Shiu and colleagues show that a relatively simple whole-brain model can produce biologically informative sensorimotor predictions. This supports reduced models as useful hypothesis generators. It does not establish that the model learns broadly, reproduces every neuronal response, or can be enlarged without recalibration. A source-code check also found that the default reproduction uses FlyWire v630; v783 is an explicit alternate configuration. [S07](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07763-9), [S40](https://github.com/philshiu/Drosophila_brain_model)

Lappalainen and colleagues combine connectomic constraints with task optimization in the visual system. The important methodological distinction is that optimized dynamics are evaluated against neural observations beyond the training objective. The available flyvis implementation uses a tiled local wiring specification and parameter sharing. Its graph is not a direct export of the entire adult brain. This makes it a useful controlled substrate, provided we distinguish reproducing the published physiological model from adapting its wiring to a generic reservoir. [S08](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07939-3), [S41](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis)

The 2026 topological-sensitivity preprint supplies relevant negative evidence. Its reported similarity across empirical and randomized connectomes questions how much topology contributes under its setup. But the inspected study uses short optimization runs, few seeds, and a limited null ensemble; sharing metadata and spectral differences complicate attribution. This is a reason to strengthen controls and convergence checks, not a demonstration that biological structure never matters. [S19](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04033v1)

Whole-brain embodiment claims need the same audit. FlyGM is a preprint with a connectomic graph embedded in locomotor control. Its methods assign glutamate and histamine excitatory roles and compare with controls whose weighting differs from the empirical graph. Those choices complicate biological and architectural interpretation. The promising existence of a controller should be separated from evidence that its precise topology caused an advantage. [S21](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17997v1)

At least three model levels remain justified. A rate model cheaply identifies normalization and resource confounds. A spiking model can test timing-dependent mechanisms if the task demands them. A physiologically fitted circuit can support biological interpretation when independent response data exist. None is universally best. Increasing detail adds unidentified parameters unless corresponding observations constrain them. The initial platform should support replacement of dynamics, not assume a single simulator embodies the scientific hypothesis.

**Implication:** establish a baseline whose stability, input mapping, readout, and optimization are understood. Validate a specific published mechanism before using it as the basis for a new intervention. Never label an adapted rate reservoir a reproduction of a physiological model.

## 3. Learning, memory, and adaptation

The mushroom body offers experimentally motivated hypotheses about sparse representations and reinforcement, without requiring a claim that it alone explains intelligence. Bennett and colleagues supply a compact reinforcement-prediction-error model and inspectable MATLAB code. It is especially suitable for validating an update rule and distinguishing feedback-dependent learning from reward-only updates. Its aggregate valence outputs should not be relabelled as identified neurons in a different larval dataset. [S10](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-021-22592-4), [S45](https://github.com/BrainsOnBoard/paper_RPEs_in_drosophila_mb)

Actual learning circuitry is richer than a single scalar teaching signal. Recurrent pathways can regulate learning; dopamine-related interactions can shape multiple memory timescales. The adult mushroom-body connectome also reveals sensory and output organization that a two-output assay deliberately omits. These sources motivate later extensions if a simple assay has insufficient retention or transfer, not an obligation to implement every mechanism first. [S30](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-020-0607-9), [S31](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07819-w), [S70](https://elifesciences.org/articles/62576)

An important counterpoint to simply adding neurons is compensatory variability. A published rate-model study finds that unconstrained heterogeneity can impair memory, while coordinated parameters or homeostasis can rescue it. Thus global population sparsity alone may hide cells that are always active or never active. Our growth assay must measure per-cell participation and representation diversity in addition to average activity. [S68](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34845010/)

Structural changes to fly learning circuits already have close computational precedent. Xie and Ocker use a larval olfactory circuit with synthetic odor classification and examine perturbations such as ablation, pruning, and rewiring. Their study narrows any claim that structural intervention itself is new. It does not establish that adult whole-brain enlargement yields flexible behavior. Differences in circuit extraction also matter: our supplementary-table audit finds 19 left MBONs with any left-KC input, whereas their modeled output selection reports 18. We will not silently force the raw table to reproduce that count. [S20](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19351)

Thesis literature supplies additional modeling context. Jürgensen's dissertation abstract describes sparse sensory representations, feedback reinforcement, higher-order learning, and connections to simulated larval behavior. It reinforces that joining mushroom-body learning to behavior is established territory. Only the repository abstract was inspected in this phase; the thesis is retained as a targeted follow-up if timing or second-order learning becomes decisive. [S69](https://kups.ub.uni-koeln.de/71836/)

Continual-learning methods from machine learning are useful controls because a biologically motivated learner must be compared with competent alternatives. A generic delta-rule learner is a cheap first comparator; regularization or consolidation methods become relevant when tasks require retention across interference. These are engineered controls, not claims of biological equivalence. [S38](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/zenke17a.html)

Recent multisensory findings suggest useful branches involving access to additional representations. But modality-specific improvement and broader adaptation are different outcomes. The olfactory-loss and spontaneous-activity preprints named in the master brief were only accessible through indexed excerpts and metadata here; their numerical and causal claims remain unresolved. They do not determine the shortlist. Visual KC inputs and a multisensory-memory preprint motivate a later transfer assay, with its evidential status kept explicit. [S58](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-49616-z), [S62](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28007)

**Implication:** measure acquisition, reversal, retention, interference, and robustness separately. Permit online learning during evaluation only under a frozen, chronological protocol. Improvement after seeing test rewards measures adaptation; it is not zero-shot transfer. A nonlearning network is a diagnostic baseline, not a sufficiently strong sole competitor.

## 4. Growth, organization, development, and evolution

Development provides evidence that enlargement need not mean uniform duplication. Elkahlah and colleagues manipulate cell populations and examine compensatory wiring, suggesting that different partners constrain convergence and divergence differently. This supports growth rules that conserve input sampling and sparse coding as hypotheses. It does not prescribe an algorithm that necessarily increases computational capacity. [S12](https://elifesciences.org/articles/52278)

Comparative Drosophila anatomy shows that related species can alter mushroom-body connectivity architecture. Broader insect comparisons associate expansion with sensory specialization. These observations motivate a portfolio of selective expansion, altered input sampling, and homeostasis, while cautioning against equating neuropil volume with general intelligence. Mammalian cellular scaling rules likewise show why neuron number and tissue volume should not be exchanged casually across species. [S13](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-48839-4), [S52](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-023-39618-8), [S39](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1805542/)

Sparse expansion-layer theory predicts tradeoffs among input degree, dimensionality, and separation. It suggests controlling the distribution of distinct PN partners when adding KCs. Exact duplicates may add parameters without adding representational diversity; independently sampled sparse features may outperform biologically conditioned ones. Both outcomes would be informative. We therefore compare cloning, conditioned resampling, and randomized growth while reporting the extra resources. [S14](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5379477/)

The graph-theoretic route must also distinguish descriptive fit from functional benefit. Network statistics and generative wiring models identify regularities or reproduce topology; they do not establish which regularities are necessary for adaptation. Anatomically plausible distance or lineage constraints may regularize a search, but their computational advantage needs held-out functional evaluation. Directed weighted developmental models are promising candidates; their current role is hypothesis generation. [S66](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07968-y), [S33](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.06795), [S34](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05021)

Artificial developmental systems such as HyperNCA offer compact rules for constructing larger networks. Open-ended evolutionary systems such as POET demonstrate a different route: changing problems and solutions together. Neither supplies direct evidence that a Drosophila-derived growing system will outperform a fixed architecture. They also introduce evaluation-budget and curriculum confounds. A later comparison should hold search evaluations fixed and test on an external task suite that the developmental process cannot select. [S32](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11674), [S35](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01753)

Module coupling is distinct from simple enlargement. The central complex provides motifs for navigation and action selection; developmental and cross-stage work provides additional organizational constraints. Coupling such a module to a learned sensory representation may increase task coverage without increasing every population. We retain this route, but defer its implementation until an input/output contract and a measurable transfer task exist. [S29](https://elifesciences.org/articles/66039), [S55](https://elifesciences.org/articles/29089), [S56](https://elifesciences.org/articles/67510v2)

**Implication:** define growth as a reproducible transformation with node lineage, changed edges, and resource consequences. A biological growth rule, a distribution matched to biological data, and an unconstrained optimizer are different experimental arms. Do not call statistical resampling artificial neurodevelopment.

## 5. Evaluation: what counts as improvement

Reservoir capacity gives an inexpensive test of memory and nonlinear transformation. Dambre's framework provides a principled way to ask which functions of input history can be recovered from a system's state. A finite task family only estimates part of that capacity; changing the readout dimension can change the measurement even when internal dynamics are unchanged. We use delayed and nonlinear targets with explicit readout controls, without presenting their sum as a universal intelligence measure. [S15](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep00514)

This route has substantial prior work. conn2res supplies an existing framework; Morra and Daley investigate connectome-derived reservoir topology. Costi and colleagues already compare fly-derived reservoirs with topology/weight hybrid controls and study time-series prediction, including a large filtered graph. Therefore, EXP-001 is a controlled validation and normalization assay, not a claim to invent fly reservoirs. Its potentially useful distinction is separating stability normalization, fixed readout access, and topology under a preregistered capacity battery. [S16](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-44900-4), [S17](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09359), [S18](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02094v2), [S67](https://www.mdpi.com/2313-7673/10/5/341)

Costi's anatomical-weight proxy and neurotransmitter-sign assumptions remain model assumptions; good forecasting does not verify those physiological choices. Their limited task family also leaves room for a broader capability profile. The Guragain preprint proposes optimizing connectomic reservoirs, but its Drosophila graph has 49 regional nodes. Comparing such a graph with differently resolved species graphs cannot establish a neuron-level scaling law. Its spectral-radius normalization also does not by itself guarantee global contraction for a nonlinear nonnormal system. [S51](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09902v1)

Statistical design is as important as task selection. Independent seeds, held-out task families, and uncertainty estimates should precede broad claims. Averages can hide tradeoffs: better reversal with worse retention is a changed profile, not unqualified improvement. The statistical-precipice work motivates careful aggregation and uncertainty in small-run comparisons. Our exact estimands and decision thresholds are specified in [BENCHMARK_SPEC.md](BENCHMARK_SPEC.md), and are project design choices rather than thresholds established by that paper. [S36](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2021/hash/f514cec81cb148559cf475e7426eed5e-Abstract.html)

Language-model scaling laws demonstrate a methodology for measuring resource relationships, not a transferable exponent for biological networks. A handful of enlarged circuits may reveal a monotonic trend or saturation; it cannot justify a universal scaling law. Record neurons, distinct edges, anatomical contact totals, free parameters, learning updates, inference operations, wall time, and memory separately. Specify which are matched in each comparison. [S37](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361)

**Implication:** keep two primary claims separate: added scale improves a specified capability; biological organization improves that capability relative to an explicit architectural control. The first can hold when the second fails. Conversely, a small biological circuit can have an advantage without a positive enlargement trend.

## 6. Implementation feasibility and reproduction

Existing embodied platforms materially reduce future engineering work. NeuroMechFly v2/FlyGym and flybody provide complementary routes to simulated bodies and control. They should be selected against a specific experimental need, not treated as evidence that the neural controller is biologically correct. The source audit found incompatible current NumPy declarations between these packages, and FlyGym documents its own FlyBody-model integration route. Use supported integration or isolated environments. [S22](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41592-024-02497-y), [S23](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09029-4), [S65](https://neuromechfly.org/tutorials/5b_using_flybody_model/)

The immediately useful software is smaller: pinned source equations, a small graph loader, sparse or dense CPU linear algebra, deterministic task generators, and a results registry. The audit records eleven repository snapshots, inspected files, licenses or missing-license status, and maintenance dates. Those checks do not establish installability or numerical reproduction. [SOFTWARE_AUDIT.md](SOFTWARE_AUDIT.md) and [REPRODUCTIONS.md](REPRODUCTIONS.md) separate those stages.

Neuromorphic implementation is a possible future optimization, not a prerequisite. The Loihi 2 preprint reports mapping a large fly model to specialized chips; it demonstrates an implementation route, not a local cost guarantee or learned-capability result. Recent online-spiking-learning work likewise deserves screening when gradient memory becomes a measured bottleneck, rather than motivating a premature hardware change. [S24](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16792), [S59](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-68453-w)

**Implication:** provision and time a bounded CPU pilot on the actual 64 GB workstation before deciding on GPU implementation. Reserve adult whole-brain downloads and body simulation for experiments that need them. The first handoffs estimate resource envelopes without pretending they are measurements.

## Coverage, remaining gaps, and decision

All six areas have substantive syntheses. Deep inspection concentrated on decision-critical methods and code: Shiu, flyvis, Bennett, Xie, the recent topology/locomotion/reservoir preprints, development, and the small reference datasets. Adjacent fields—transcriptomics, neuromorphic systems, predictive learning, artificial development, evolution, comparative anatomy, and thesis literature—were screened at explicitly shallower levels. A full thesis review, exhaustive forward-citation census, and validation of inaccessible 2026 preprint methods remain gaps. Publication status of older arXiv versions should be rechecked before citing them in a paper; the bibliography labels the version actually inspected.

The late identification of Costi and compensatory-variability work tightened novelty language and homeostasis diagnostics. The subsequent targeted rounds described in the search log did not change the shortlist. We therefore stop this foundation survey at a bounded decision point and move next to a selected reproduction and pilot. Reopen research when a pilot exposes an unexplained effect, a resource assumption fails, or new evidence changes the branch ranking.
