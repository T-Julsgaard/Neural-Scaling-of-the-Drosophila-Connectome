# Bounded participation calibration and readable but forgotten information in connectome-derived mushroom-body models

Local empirical report draft — 16 September 2026; revised after attribution/content review. For scientific review, not submitted or published. Author list, affiliations and venue are unset. Figures remain specified in the [figure plan](SESSION_D_FIGURE_PLAN.md). The [Session D assessment](SESSION_D_ASSESSMENT.md) contains the claim/evidence audit; the [content and attribution record](REPORT_CONTENT_AND_ATTRIBUTION.md) identifies direct dependencies and result priorities.

## Abstract

Improved activity balance need not improve the memories retained by a particular learner. We evaluated bounded, frozen participation calibration in normalized feedforward projections derived from larval and adult Drosophila mushroom-body anatomy. Relative to ordinary tuning with equal numbers of rewarded candidate settings, calibration changed final associative-retention probability by -0.27 percentage points in the larval model (98.333333% interval -1.67 to 1.13) and -0.19 in the adult model (-1.01 to 0.68), within a predeclared +/-3-point practical-equivalence band. Worst-memory noninferiority was not established. Subsequent larval diagnostics showed that useful old distinctions remained readable by offline linear fits despite loss under the tested sequential readout. A prospective two-context XOR test supported this restricted prediction, with a 43.95-point offline–sequential old-context accuracy gap (95% interval 34.23 to 53.67). Random sparse representations performed similarly offline, and a larger quadratic representation retained both contexts sequentially. Calibration also had a secondary positive retention effect on XOR. These findings delimit one calibration procedure and learner family; they do not show universal failure of homeostasis, a new forgetting mechanism, anatomical superiority or a biological memory limit.

## Motivation and relation to prior work

This study asks whether a practical participation-balancing intervention adds useful retention beyond ordinary parameter tuning in two extracted projections, and whether failed retention necessarily reflects unavailable feature information. The motivation arose from earlier local active-count benefits and persistent-memory failures; the negative calibration result was not the original desired outcome.

Compensation-related memory benefits already have a direct computational precedent in [Abdelrahman, Vasilaki and Lin (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2102158118). Sparse expansion and output-layer continual learning are likewise addressed by [Zou, Zang and Ji (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01427), and streaming ridge classification appears in the later [Fly-CL work](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16877v1). [Expansion/connectivity tradeoffs](https://columbia.edu/cu/neurotheory/Larry/KumarNeuron17.pdf) and [rehearsal against forgetting](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/09540099550039318) are established background. Our contribution is the bounded empirical comparison and diagnostic evidence, not these principles. The [literature audit](SESSION_D_LITERATURE_AUDIT.md) distinguishes source versions and reading depth; no priority claim follows from the targeted search.

## Models and experimental design

The larval contact matrix comes from Supplementary Table 1 of [Eichler et al. (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature23455); our extracted projection contains 40 inputs and 73 mature Kenyon-cell-derived features. The adult model uses the hemibrain v1.1 export associated with [Scheffer et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.57443), with mushroom-body anatomical context from [Li et al. (2020)](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.62576). We use the monoglomerular PN list distributed in [Abdelrahman and colleagues' pinned repository](https://github.com/aclinlab/CompensatoryVariability/tree/9f3f7e9e85117febef1ad32e3152c830570f74d3). Our adult extraction selects positive main-calyx CA(R) contacts to KCg-m, yielding 104 connected PNs, 590 KCs, 4,878 neuron-pair edges and 85,151 contacts. Subset extraction and per-KC normalization are our transformations of these authors' anatomical resources; exact versions, hashes and exclusions are in the [data-provenance record](../DATA_PROVENANCE.md).

Each feature's incoming weights sum to one. Anatomical counts do not specify physiological synaptic strengths; inputs are synthetic coordinates, including independent coordinates for adult PNs sharing a glomerular identity. Outputs are two synthetic opponent readouts. There is no recurrent circuit, measured MBON dynamics, dopamine model or new biological experiment.

The learning-rule development began with [Bennett, Philippides and Nowotny (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-021-22592-4). EXP-002 implemented their mixed-valence Eq. 8 and checked it against the [pinned author code](https://github.com/BrainsOnBoard/paper_RPEs_in_drosophila_mb/tree/7ec52afb9bd7bb748d94d60dea9f483645a2ce8e). Subsequent studies use the separately tested generic opponent delta learner: for feature vector x, error e = y - x·(w+ - w-), update w+ to max(0,w+ + eta*x*e) and w- to max(0,w- - eta*x*e). This is not the complete Bennett feedback model. The author-equation check validates that initial implementation, not a biological interpretation of the later generic rule.

Top-K selection produces constant amplitude 10/K per winner. Motivated by the compensation work of [Abdelrahman, Vasilaki and Lin](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2102158118), we implement a distinct bounded partial-participation procedure: calibration subtracts a fitted offset before selection. Separate 4,096-observation unlabeled fit and validation pools precede rewarded development. Targets partly interpolate baseline participation toward K/N, with strengths .25 or .5; centered offsets are capped at one-quarter of the median positive drive standard deviation and fitted for 100 steps. They remain fixed during evaluation. The target, bound and fixed-amplitude encoder are project design choices. This is residual partial calibration after incoming-weight normalization, not full activity equalization or online biological homeostasis.

EXP-007 and EXP-008 each allocate 32 rewarded candidate settings per method per development condition, but their search spaces differ: ordinary tuning varies winner count, rate and temperature; calibrated methods vary strength, rate and temperature at the higher winner count. Larval ordinary selects K6; adult ordinary selects K32 while adult calibration uses K48. Null projections preserve specified degrees/contact multisets, and shuffled-offset controls test assignment. Three fixed null/sham instances are averaged within task blocks, not counted as independent biological replicates. Calibration and replica evaluation add costs beyond candidate count.

Persistent associative sequences use noisy paired cues and stochastic .8/.2 outcomes, with updates using only chosen outcomes. Primary retention is mean preferred-choice probability after 16 similar pairs and 128 presentations per pair. Fresh confirmation comprises 32 larval and 24 adult blocks. Paired block bootstrap intervals use 98.333333% coverage for three primary contrasts; equivalence requires the entire interval inside +/-3 points. Twelve separate adaptation/tail guardrails test noninferiority. These are approximate conditional intervals, not animal-population inference.

Session A directly adapts the supplied fitted model and SI Eqs. 3, 20 and 21 from [Abdelrahman et al.](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2102158118), including their supplied PN table and empirical-noise lookup, on fresh tasks with equal rate-search opportunities and author-runtime arithmetic checks. Original files and notices are retained; [the source audit](SESSION_A_SOURCE_AUDIT.md) separates inherited parameters/equations from our new task draws and training-only scaling. This direct reuse is distinct from the conceptual precedent for our partial-offset procedure.

Session B used fresh larval pair-memory blocks to compare chosen-outcome and full-outcome ridge fits against matching-access online procedures. Ridge penalties were development-selected; online eta remained fixed. Full-feedback arms receive 4,096 observations/outcomes versus 2,048 chosen updates. Offline access to stored history and a different optimization objective makes it a diagnostic, not an equal-resource competitor. Zou and colleagues' studies are related-work comparisons, not implementations or trained parameters reused in these experiments.

Session C froze a two-context XOR task before evaluation. The contexts reverse the association of the second component with the label; continuous strengths, nuisance channels and noise generate fresh observations. All four logical combinations occur in training. It tests nonlinear discrimination of new observations, not unseen logical combinations. The same 256 observations and exact labels feed all learners. Four learning rates per representation/schedule and four ridge penalties are selected on six development blocks; 24 fresh confirmation blocks remain fixed. A quadratic direct-input representation validates learnability, while direct linear input is a negative control. The primary conjunction requires native/calibrated average offline old accuracy >=80%, a lower 95% bound above 5 points for the offline–blocked gap, and a lower bound above 5 points for acquisition-to-final loss. The first requirement is a mean criterion, not a lower-bound criterion.

## Results

**Bounded calibration did not add useful mean associative retention.** Larval calibrated retention was 79.28%, compared with 79.55% for ordinary tuning; the difference was -0.27 [-1.67, 1.13] points. The adult difference was -0.19 [-1.01, 0.68]. Both intervals fit the prospective practical-equivalence band. Each experiment passed 9/12 guardrails, with worst-memory preservation unestablished against all three comparators. This does not demonstrate harm, nor does mean equivalence certify individual memories. For example, larval ordinary tuning's 79.55% mean coexisted with a mean within-sequence worst-pair probability of 13.54% and 18.36% of pairs below chance. These are different summaries of the same condition, not contradictory estimates. Larval unused presented cells declined from 40.88% to 35.66%; greater recruitment was insufficient for the intended behavioral gain. The intervention remained partial: 49.32% of selected larval offsets reached their bound. Direct inputs remained strong descriptive baselines with fewer reward-learned weights. Figure 1 will show the primary effects, participation and tails together.

**The supplied-model positive control helped but did not create a causal bridge.** A's compensated-minus-uncompensated correct-valence probability was +8.58 [8.13, 9.03] points, with a 95% paired task-block interval. It used one author-fitted network, not a regenerated twenty-network ensemble. Saved compensated excitation was fivefold, whereas the inspected script specified 4.5-fold. Gain, inhibition, thresholds, response norms, tasks and learning differ from our partial-offset system. This positive control therefore neither validates the upstream optimizer nor identifies why our earlier calibration result was null.

**Useful information remained readable under matched feedback.** In B, offline full-feedback old-pair accuracy averaged 96.97% and supervised sequential accuracy 76.72%, a paired 20.26 [16.46, 24.05]-point gap. Acquisition-to-final loss was 23.23 [19.02, 27.45]. Chosen-only fits also exceeded their matching chosen-feedback learner. High-noise offline accuracy declined to 84.49%, so the data do not establish universally intact representations. Removing clipping and replaying old observations helped, but did not uniquely separate objective, convergence and schedule. B establishes a limitation of the specified procedure, with asymmetric tuning opportunities explicitly retained.

**Readable but forgotten information also occurred on the nonlinear compound task.** In C, offline old-context accuracy was 95.67 [92.91, 98.44]%, versus 51.73 [41.24, 62.21]% for the tuned blocked learner. The gap was 43.95 [34.23, 53.67] points and acquisition-to-final loss 45.86 [35.78, 55.93]. All frozen criteria passed. The paired-gap interval was wider than the planning example; no sample extension occurred. Selected blocked rates were the lowest in the four-rate grid. Zero clipping events and equal selected unclipped scores show that clipping is unnecessary for this particular deficit.

Counterevidence limits interpretation. Random sparse offline old accuracy was 95.89%. Quadratic features achieved 100% observed old accuracy under the tuned sequential procedure, although their dimension was 861 versus 73. Direct linear failure was expected on XOR and cannot establish anatomical superiority. Calibration improved sequential old accuracy by 12.91 [5.56, 20.25] points in a secondary unadjusted comparison, with new accuracy changing +0.52 [-1.55, 2.59]; its offline difference was only -0.22 [-1.85, 1.41]. Thus calibration is not uniformly ineffective, and C does not fully explain the earlier nulls. Figure 2 must retain these results.

**Historical access mattered beyond update count.** At the common eta=1/150 and 2,560 updates, C replay10 exceeded local10 old accuracy by 56.68 [46.97, 66.39] points while new accuracy changed -0.89 [-1.44, -0.33]. These descriptive intervals compare the same unique examples, labels, update count and rate; stored-history availability and ordering change jointly. Shuffled one-pass training also improved retention but requires simultaneous access to both contexts. Separately tuned schedule results additionally conflate rate selection with schedule. Figure 3 will show old and new outcomes alongside access and update costs.

## Interpretation and limitations

The evidence demonstrates that the tested online procedures can fail to retain distinctions that fitted readouts recover from frozen features. It does not establish a capacity upper bound, optimal streaming performance, a unique mediation pathway or the cause of the published/local difference. Native/calibrated averaging is the prospective estimand, not evidence that each condition has identical effects. Task blocks condition on one larval circuit, one adult circuit where applicable, fixed nulls and fitted offsets; many probes do not increase the anatomical sample size. B/C diagnostics were not repeated in the adult circuit.

C is a deliberately small interference challenge. The quadratic counterexample confines its result to a feature/procedure interaction. Direct/quadratic scale normalization also uses all training inputs, including future-context inputs, though no held-out probes or labels enter fitting. The simulator stores paired datasets for reproducibility; procedural restrictions on revisiting examples are not measured streaming-memory savings. Ridge has simultaneous-history and computational privileges. No efficiency ranking follows from aggregate block timings.

Our prospective freezes are local provenance records, not external preregistration. Historical studies motivated later questions. The EXP-007 exact-sham addition followed development but preceded confirmation; duplicate selected contrasts do not provide independent replication. A's historical preservation scan completed after confirmation, and C's preservation audit covered a documented subset. Automated equation checks, checksum verification and deterministic replay support reproducibility but are not independent scientific validation.

The contribution is the measured outcome of a specified partial intervention under ordinary-tuning controls, its prospective adult follow-up, and the subsequent larval diagnostics. For researchers building comparable models, the practical implication is to measure old-memory behavior, new learning and individual-memory losses alongside participation and decoding, while recording which history a learner can access. The underlying principles are established; these controlled measurements delimit their application here.

The positive secondary calibration result on XOR is part of this contribution's boundary, not an inconvenient exception. It prevents interpreting the associative practical null as a general verdict. The experiments differ in tasks, labels, tuning and endpoints, so their signs alone do not establish a formal task-by-calibration interaction or identify the cause of the difference. We also have not tested the stronger claim that sequential forgetting masks a compensation benefit on the original task. That would require a matched intervention-by-learning-procedure comparison with appropriate inference.
