# Report content and attribution decisions

16 September 2026, follow-up to Session D. Read against the draft, experiment reports/protocols, data/software provenance, reproduction register and actual learner/calibration code. This is an editorial and attribution review; no new experimental evidence.

## Recommended central message

**In these simplified connectome-derived models, improved participation statistics did not guarantee better retained behavior, and poor sequential retention did not imply that the frozen features lacked readable task information.** These are bounded measurements. Their conjunction is the useful report, not a new law of memory or an explanation of all compensation results.

The paper should answer three questions in order: what intervention was tested, what happened to retained behavior, and what the readout diagnostics do and do not explain. Avoid narrating every project session or making the largest effect size the headline.

## What is ours and why a reader might care

The new contribution is the measured outcome of the specified partial intervention under strong ordinary controls, its adult follow-up, and the subsequent prospective diagnostic evidence with counterexamples retained. A reader building a comparable model learns that more recruited units and good offline decoding are insufficient performance criteria; final old-memory behavior, new learning, tail losses and permitted historical access must also be measured. That is a useful empirical lesson within scope, even though its underlying principles are familiar.

State the C calibration benefit beside the earlier practical nulls. It is scientifically important precisely because it prevents an overly simple conclusion. The report should stop at “these regimes give different observed calibration outcomes”; it has not identified which changed factor causes the difference.
